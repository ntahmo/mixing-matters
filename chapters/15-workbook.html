Exercises and worked answers

Practice the decisions that catch the most common mistakes.

Exercise 1 — opportunity totals

Group A has N_A=80 people with c_A=5 ends/person/year. Group B has N_B=300 and c_B=1. Find Q_A, Q_B, and their proportionate partner shares.

Answer and reasoning

Q_A = 400; Q_B = 300; Q = 700. Both source rows assign 400/700 ≈ 57.14% to A and 300/700 ≈ 42.86% to B. Source populations alone would give the wrong proportions.

Exercise 2 — reciprocity

Suppose Q_A=400, Q_B=100, and M_AB=0.10. Which M_BA balances between-group partnership ends?

Answer and reasoning

The A→B flow is 40. B must send 40 ends to A, requiring M_BA = 40/100 = 0.40. The mixing probabilities need not be equal.

Exercise 3 — zero preference with correlated strata

Suppose Q_HP=80, Q_LP=20, Q_HV=20, Q_LV=80. Find the correct proportionate destination probability for HP and compare it with the product of marginal high-activity and physical-context shares.

Answer and reasoning

Q = 200, so the correct joint probability is 80/200 = 0.40. High activity accounts for 100/200 and physical context for 100/200, so their marginal product is 0.25. The difference arises from association between activity and context.

Exercise 4 — why symmetry alone fails

Start with Q_A=100, Q_B=200, M_AB=M_BA=0.40. The cross-flows are 40 and 80. Suppose someone averages them to 60 in both directions but leaves within-group flow totals unchanged. What happens?

Answer and reasoning

The cross-flows become symmetric, but each group's total flow changes by ±20. The row sums no longer equal Q_A and Q_B unless all other flows are adjusted as part of a constrained balancing construction.

Exercise 5 — interpret ε

In standard preferred mixing ε=0.3, and group A contributes 60% of opportunities. What is M_AA? Is it equal to 30%?

Answer and reasoning

M_AA = 0.3 + (1−0.3)×0.6 = 0.72. The observed within-group probability is 72%, not 30%; ε is the mixing-component weight.

Exercise 6 — structure before calculation

You have detailed age-specific contact diaries but no partner duration information. Should you begin with an age mixing matrix or a dynamic partnership network?

Answer and reasoning

An age-specific contact matrix is the better-supported starting point. A dynamic partnership network may still be scientifically useful, but its duration and dissolution parameters would need additional evidence and sensitivity analyses.

Exercise 7 — a prompt you should reject

An AI assistant claims that multiplying separately normalized activity and typology kernels is always reciprocal because every row sums to one. What is the logical error?

Answer and reasoning

Row normalization is not reciprocity. Reciprocity compares Q_i M_ij with Q_j M_ji. A row-stochastic product construction can fail this equality, especially with correlated joint opportunities or unequal opportunity totals.
