Technical derivations and proofs

For readers who want to verify—not merely accept—the algebra behind the teaching constructions.

1. Define the model system precisely

Index joint groups by i=(r,m). Let Qᵢ>0 denote partnership ends supplied per unit time by group i; set Q=ΣᵢQᵢ. Let M be a nonnegative row-stochastic matrix of partner-group probabilities, and F=diag(Q)M its flow matrix.

Σⱼ Mᵢⱼ = 1   ⇔   Σⱼ Fᵢⱼ = Qᵢ

For reciprocal undirected partnerships, impose Fᵢⱼ=Fⱼᵢ. Thus the mathematical task is to construct a symmetric nonnegative flow matrix with prescribed row margins. This framing converts “why does reciprocity keep failing?” into a standard matrix-construction problem.

2. Proportionate mixing is reciprocal

Choose Fᵢⱼ=QᵢQⱼ/Q. The row total is Σⱼ QᵢQⱼ/Q=Qᵢ, and Fᵢⱼ=Fⱼᵢ. Therefore Mᵢⱼ=Qⱼ/Q satisfies both normalization and reciprocity.

3. Exact-group preferred mixing is reciprocal

For 0≤ε≤1, choose Fᵢⱼ = ε Qᵢ1(i=j)+(1−ε)QᵢQⱼ/Q. The diagonal term is symmetric, and the proportionate term is symmetric. Row totals are εQᵢ+(1−ε)Qᵢ=Qᵢ, hence Mᵢⱼ = ε1(i=j)+(1−ε)Qⱼ/Q.

4. When does a product of two one-axis kernels work?

Suppose the joint opportunity distribution factorizes exactly into independent margins:

Qᵣₘ/Q = (Qᵣ/Q)(Qₘ/Q)

Let Aᵣᵣ′ be a row-stochastic activity kernel reciprocal with respect to activity margins Qᵣ, and let Bₘₘ′ be a row-stochastic typology kernel reciprocal with respect to typology margins Qₘ. Define the product kernel Mᵣₘ,ᵣ′ₘ′ = Aᵣᵣ′ Bₘₘ′.

Because the joint group weight is QᵣQₘ/Q, the forward flow becomes:

Qᵣₘ Mᵣₘ,ᵣ′ₘ′ = (QᵣAᵣᵣ′)(QₘBₘₘ′)/Q

Each parenthesized factor is symmetric under reversing its own indices. Their product is therefore symmetric, so the joint kernel is reciprocal. Normalization also follows because Σᵣ′Σₘ′Aᵣᵣ′Bₘₘ′=(Σᵣ′Aᵣᵣ′)(Σₘ′Bₘₘ′)=1.

Precise conclusion: the product of two marginal preferred-mixing kernels is a valid reciprocal construction when the joint opportunity distribution factorizes and the constituent kernels are themselves reciprocal with respect to the correct margins. Without those conditions, do not assume validity.

For the correlated teaching distribution Q=[400,200,200,300] and ε=0.4 in both marginal kernels, the product formula gives HP→LP ≈79.34 ends/year but LP→HP ≈47.60 ends/year. Rows still sum to one. This concrete counterexample shows that normalization alone does not guarantee reciprocity.

5. A symmetric construction for correlated groups

If the opportunity distribution does not factorize, one possible construction places positive symmetric affinities on each potential group pair:

Wᵢⱼ=(QᵢQⱼ/Q) exp[θᵣ1(rᵢ=rⱼ)+θₘ1(mᵢ=mⱼ)]

We seek positive factors aᵢ such that Fᵢⱼ=aᵢWᵢⱼaⱼ and ΣⱼFᵢⱼ=Qᵢ. If the numerical solver reaches the requested tolerance, F is symmetric by construction and has the correct margins; Mᵢⱼ=Fᵢⱼ/Qᵢ is consequently reciprocal and row-stochastic. This is a prescribed-margin scaling problem related to the general theory developed by Rothblum & Schneider (1989).

At θᵣ=θₘ=0, W=QᵢQⱼ/Q already has margins Q, so aᵢ=1 works and the baseline reduces exactly to proportionate mixing. The θ values are not probabilities or ε values; θ>0 increases relative unbalanced weight for matching that dimension. Once margins are enforced, the realized matching proportions reflect the entire joint population.

6. Why “just symmetrize the flows” is insufficient

Given an arbitrary flow matrix G, define H=(G+Gᵀ)/2. H is symmetric, but in general ΣⱼHᵢⱼ≠Qᵢ. The original group partnership-rate totals are no longer respected. Further row normalization would break symmetry. A valid balancing method must satisfy both types of constraints simultaneously.

7. Dynamic model implication

If Qᵢ(t)=Nᵢ(t)cᵢ(t) changes with time, a fixed M may cease to be reciprocal. Recompute or otherwise adapt the flows whenever the matching margins change, or use an explicit partner formation/dissolution model with its own mechanics. The relevant mathematical consistency check depends on what the modeled encounters mean and which populations are inside the boundary. See Arregui et al. (2018).

8. A reproducible numerical diagnostic

F = diag(Q) @ M
assert all(abs(M.sum(axis=1) - 1) < tol)
assert all(M >= 0)
assert max(abs(F - F.T)) < tol_flow

The short code above is pseudocode using NumPy-like matrix notation. Working dependency-free implementations and tests are available in Resources. Always report tolerances relative to the flow scale, especially when some groups offer many more partnership opportunities than others.

Limitations: these proofs apply to the specified row-stochastic partner-share setup with positive opportunities and undirected partnership ends. Directed contacts, external partners, heterogeneous pair capacities, structured pair formation, and integer realization require adaptations.
